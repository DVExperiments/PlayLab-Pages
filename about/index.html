Futures PlayLab

The Futures PlayLab supports hands-on experimentation, creative practice and critical engagement with technological futures. Rather than operating as a conventional technology lab, it provides a facilitated environment where learners explore, make, test, break, reflect and adapt.

A growing academyEX initiative

The PlayLab is designed to create the conditions for experimentation, creative practice and critical engagement with emerging technologies. It's built around developing futures, creative, digital and adaptive capability, and helps learners connect technological possibilities with real professional, organisational, cultural and community contexts.

It was first developed alongside the refreshed Master of Technological Futures (MTF), as one of the practical environments where the programme's ideas are explored and put into action.

You don't need to be technical

You don't need to code. You need curiosity and the ambition to experiment. Software access alone isn't enough, so the PlayLab pairs tools with facilitation, support and a community to learn with.

Master of Technological Futures

A professional master's for experienced people ready to lead technological change.

Programme page on academyex.com

The MTF develops adaptive leaders who can critically understand, creatively shape and responsibly lead technological change within complex human, cultural, organisational and ecological systems.

High-end thinking, creatively grounded through adaptive practice. The programme combines futures and systems thinking with emerging technologies and responsible innovation, grounded in contextual inquiry and applied practice.

Why this programme?

Work is shifting as AI and emerging technology change how organisations create value. The MTF is for experienced professionals ready to move past technical awareness into the fluency to lead through that change without losing their footing.

For current leaders

Lead with fluency.

Move from reacting to technology change to being the person who decides what your organisation does with it.

For emerging leaders

Step up with credentials.

Gain the qualification and confidence to be trusted with bigger, more technical decisions.

For non-technical professionals

Curiosity, not code.

You don't need to code. You need curiosity and the ambition to experiment. The programme builds the fluency, not the syntax.

Contextual Adaptation

Bring Your Own Context

Contextual Adaptation is the thread running through the whole programme. It connects advanced theory and technology to your own professional, organisational, cultural, community, historical and ecological setting. You bring a live context into the programme, and Part 1 deliberately challenges your assumptions before a project direction is fixed.

  1. Advanced theory and technological insight
  2. Critical and creative interpretation
  3. Contextual inquiry
  4. Design and experimentation
  5. Adaptive intervention
  6. Evaluation
  7. Reflexive learning and renewal

The process loops rather than running in a straight line: interventions change contexts, contexts change interventions, and you revise both your actions and your understanding.

Our five pillars

The pillars give the programme a lasting structure while technologies and cases change. Choose a pillar to see the question at its heart.

The pillars don't map one-to-one onto courses. Each course has a primary anchor and supporting pillars, and all five spiral through the programme. The Part 3 project brings all five together.

The learner journey

180 credits across three parts. Each part builds on the last, moving from guided exploration to independent, work-based research.

Our values

The MTF is grounded in Te Ara Kōtihi, academyEX's values-based teaching and learning framework. It puts the learner at the centre, supported by people, structures and processes that together create a pathway to success.

Our tohu

Te Ara Kōtihi

A working group was formed and consultation was undertaken, with students, staff and people we collaborate with. Our tohu, which already existed as a physical pin for each of our graduating students, became a visual representation of how we teach and learn.

It represents what we strive for at academyEX:

  • KoruOur students at the centre.
  • Niho taniwhaOur structures, people and processes, all linked together to support the learner.
  • KōtihiThe summit shape enclosing everything else: together, forming a pathway to success.

Our four values

Self-determination

Rangatiratanga

We recognise diverse talents, identities, contexts and ways of learning, and support you to shape relevant inquiry, choose how you express your work and have a real say in technological futures.

Care

Manaaki

We create inclusive, supportive environments where you can experiment, take intellectual and creative risks, receive feedback and build confidence and capability.

Reciprocal teaching and learning

Ako

Learners, advisors, academics, communities and professional contributors all bring different expertise. Knowledge moves through dialogue, collaboration, studio practice and peer learning.

Transparency and integrity

Pono

We engage honestly and responsibly with evidence, technology, research, AI, communities and one another, making decisions, assumptions and impacts visible and open to reflection.

Shaped collectively

Technological futures are shaped collectively. The goal isn't only individual advancement, but knowledge, capability and practices that enrich organisations, professions, iwi, hapū, communities and wider Aotearoa. The programme keeps an explicit focus on Māori and Pacific learner success, and brings in concepts such as whanaungatanga, kaitiakitanga, whakapapa, Indigenous data sovereignty and plural futures where relevant.
